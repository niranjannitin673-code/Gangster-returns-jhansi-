# Story Bible — Gangster Returns: Jhansi

**Source screenplay:** *Robin: The Dark Throne* by Nitin Patel (29 scenes). The screenplay is the source for the characters, dramatic turns and chapter titles. This document is an adaptation plan, not a replacement screenplay.

## Locked setting decision

The entire game campaign takes place in **Jhansi, Uttar Pradesh**. There is no Mumbai prologue. Every Mumbai location named in the screenplay is to be re-staged as a Jhansi location or a clearly fictional site inside the Jhansi game world. Keep the screenplay's core conflict and character relationships; do not simply rename Mumbai streets while retaining coastal geography.

## Main dramatic spine

Robin begins as a hidden strategist seeking revenge for his father's death. He builds a loyal network, uses information and carefully staged pressure to turn rival criminal and political powers against one another, and gradually gains public influence. His rise becomes morally unsettling: the player should feel the cost of Robin's methods, not just celebrate his victories. The final movement should preserve the screenplay's ominous victory/curse tone and sequel hook.

## Character continuity

Preserve the screenplay's named characters and roles: Robin, Zed (Zeeshan), Shammi, Ranveer Thakur, Raja Bhau, Milind Kale, Nishant, Jadhav, and the political/criminal figures introduced in the script. Do not silently merge characters or change who serves whom. Robin is a strategist first; fear, surveillance, leverage and public perception are central to his style.

## Jhansi world design

The game uses a compressed, fictionalized open-world version of Jhansi. Landmark silhouettes and street atmosphere can be inspired by real places, but mission interiors, warehouses, hideouts, alleys and criminal businesses are fictional. Do not imply that real people or businesses are connected to fictional crimes.

### Landmark anchors

- **Jhansi Fort / Bangra hill:** skyline landmark and visual compass; a high-ground vantage point for story observation scenes. The fort's historic gates can inspire fictional access points, but restricted heritage areas are not gameplay crime locations.
- **Rani Mahal / old city:** heritage architecture and dense old-city street atmosphere; use respectfully as a visual reference, not as a criminal hideout by default.
- **Laxmi Darwaza and Laxmi Tal:** old-city transition and waterside route; useful for navigation, chase routes and establishing shots.
- **Jhansi Railway Station / freight-side fictional yards:** transport hub, rail ambience and a fictional industrial/warehouse belt. Any warehouse or basement used by the story is invented.
- **Government Museum / park belt:** civic and public-space atmosphere for scenes about public opinion, evidence and political image.
- **Garhmau Lake / outer countryside:** quieter outskirts for surveillance, escape routes and later open-world exploration.

These real-world anchors are based on official Jhansi district and Uttar Pradesh tourism descriptions. The playable map is not a survey-accurate replica; distances and road links will be compressed for mobile gameplay.

## Gameplay adaptation guardrails

- Build missions around exploration, driving, surveillance, evidence collection, dialogue choices, faction pressure and consequences.
- Do not turn Robin into a straightforward heroic crime-fighter. The moral cost of his rise is essential.
- Do not reproduce screenplay dialogue verbatim in the game without a deliberate adaptation pass.
- Keep the city fictionalized and respectful: real heritage sites are landmarks, not endorsements of fictional criminal activity.
- Start with a small, performant district slice; expand the world after Android profiling.
